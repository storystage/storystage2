# 8. The curriculum

StoryStage has been mapped against the curricula in force in all four UK nations: 156 curriculum statements, 95 of them a strong fit. The full mapping, with every statement cited to the official documents, is published under CC BY 4.0, so you can copy it into your own planning. **[Link: the published curriculum pages; in the repository, `docs/curriculum/`.]**

## Where it fits best

| Nation | The strongest fits |
|---|---|
| **England** | English from Year 1 to KS3 (retelling tales; settings, characters and plot; dialogue; performing; Shakespeare's staging); computing at KS1–KS2 (algorithms, debugging, sequence, selection, repetition, variables) |
| **Scotland** | Computing science at First and Second level (predicting a visual program, parallel actions, finding where a program doesn't match the task); literacy and English, where a film counts as a text; drama |
| **Wales** | Expressive Arts, whose disciplines include film and digital media; languages, literacy and communication; computation; the Digital Competence Framework (creating, planning, evaluating, and digital rights and licensing) |
| **Northern Ireland** | Using ICT (film and animation, and coding, are both named); KS3 English with Media Education; drama; the coding in KS1–KS2 maths |

History, science and art fit partly everywhere: StoryStage helps learners tell or show what they know, and the substance is yours to teach. The clearest exceptions are the Earth and space (England Year 5; Scotland `SCN 2-06a`) and recreating a historical setting (Scotland `SOC 1-04a`).

The curricula of England, Scotland and Northern Ireland change from 2027–28; the mapping is redone as each is published.

## Story templates

When learners plan a film, they choose a story shape. Each part has a question on screen that helps them think, and the story coach (if the Prompter is on) asks the same questions without writing the answers.

| Template | Parts | Ages |
|---|---|---|
| 3-part | Beginning, middle, end | Any |
| 5-part | Set-up, conflict, challenge, climax, resolution | Any |
| **Traditional tale** | Once upon a time, a problem, three tries, it works, happily ever after | 6–9 |
| **Myth, legend or quest** | The call, the journey, the trial, the return | 8–13 |
| **A scene from a play** | Entrance, the clash, turning point, exit | 9–13 |
| **Explain how it works** | The question, what we see, why it happens, what it means | 8–13 |

A tip: the traditional tale's "three tries" is a natural place to teach a **count** loop.
