# 10. Questions teachers ask

**Is it free?**
Yes. StoryStage is free and open source. The only cost is the optional Prompter, which the adult who owns the Claude key pays Anthropic for, and which a monthly spend limit and the class daily limit keep in hand.

**Do pupils need accounts or email addresses?**
No. There are no accounts, and StoryStage has no server.

**Does it work offline?**
The apps do, apart from the Prompter. The first time voice input is used, the app downloads its speech model once. The browser version needs the internet to open.

**Which devices?**
Windows, Mac, iPad and Chromebook apps, and a browser version for anything else, including Chromebooks where Android apps are blocked. [Get StoryStage](../download.md)

**What ages is it for?**
Six to 13. The youngest learners need an adult nearby for reading and for their first lessons; guided films, read-aloud tips and voice input help.

**Can pupils carry on at home?**
Yes: a production is a `.storystage` file that opens in any version, and the browser version needs nothing installed. **[Open: how pupils take their work home from shared school devices follows the decision on where work lives (WP24.2).]**

**Can my class use it without the Prompter?**
Yes. Every lesson and use case works with it off, and the browser version doesn't have it at all.

**Does the Prompter write the story for them?**
No, never. It asks questions about the story, explains, and suggests plans for building, which the learner checks before anything changes. [The Prompter](05-the-prompter.md)

**Is it safe?**
The Prompter is off until an adult sets it up; teachers choose how much of it each class gets, and schools can lock it. It sees text only, never pictures or voices, and it is told not to ask for personal details. Nothing a learner makes is sent to StoryStage. [Privacy](../privacy.md)

**Can we use Alice worlds?**
Yes: StoryStage 2 opens Alice 3 worlds (`.a3p` files), and keeps Alice's way of coding. A school can let imported worlds be coded before they are storyboarded.

**Why can't we use Paddington, or Winnie-the-Pooh?**
They are still protected by copyright or trade marks. Backstage includes characters from stories that are free to use: myths, legends, fairy tales and Shakespeare. [Backstage and licences](07-backstage-and-licences.md) **[Open: the final list follows the rights decisions (WP17.8).]**

**Can we share films online?**
Yes, following your school's consent arrangements, and with first names or initials in the credits. StoryStage blocks public export of a film that uses a model whose licence doesn't allow it, and says why.

**Is StoryStage available in Welsh?**
Not yet. English comes first, and Welsh is the first language to follow, in the release after the pilot.
