**Subject:** Permission to use Alice 3 gallery models in StoryStage 2, a free tool for schools

Dear Alice team,

I lead StoryStage, a free, open-source tool that helps young people aged 6 to 13 in UK schools and clubs make short 3D animated films. They plan a story on a storyboard, set each scene and program it in blocks, then screen and export the film. StoryStage 1, in use today, is a fork of Alice 3 and keeps Alice's licence and notices.

We are now building StoryStage 2 on web technology (Apache-2.0, at github.com/storystage/storystage2). It contains no Alice code, but it keeps Alice's vocabulary and its approach to teaching programming through storytelling. We would very much like learners to keep Alice's gallery characters, animals and props too.

Clause 5 of the Alice 3 licence says the gallery was contributed by Electronic Arts and may be used for personal, non-commercial and academic use only. Before we use any model, we would be grateful for your help with these questions:

1. **Which models did CMU make, or hold the rights to?** The attached list has all 435 models in the gallery. We will not use anything drawn from The Sims 2's art, including the Alice 3 people and two Bamboo variants.
2. **For those models, may we:**
    - convert them to glTF and simplify them so they run on low-cost school devices;
    - include them in StoryStage 2, which is free and has no accounts, adverts or data collection, on Windows, macOS, iPad, Chromebook and in the browser;
    - let learners use them in the films they make, and share those films (video files and simple AR scenes) outside school, for example with family or at a film festival?
3. **How should we credit CMU** on screen and in film credits?

We are happy to answer any questions, and to show you the project. Thank you for Alice and for considering this.

Yours sincerely,

[Name]
StoryStage
