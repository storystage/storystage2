- **Checked:** 25 October 2026. UK law (copyright: the author's life plus 70 years, ending on 31 December). Not legal advice. **No trade mark register search has been done** (the UK IPO register blocks automated searches); the "Register check" column says what the owner or attorney should look up before commissioning.

## 1. How to read this

Three kinds of rights can survive after a story is out of copyright:

1. **Copyright in later pictures and adaptations.** An old text can be free while a later illustrator's drawings, or a film's designs, are still protected (Pooh: Milne's text is free in the UK from 1 January 2027, but Shepard's drawings are protected until 1 January 2047). A design copied from a protected film or illustration infringes, even if the story is free.
2. **Registered trade marks** in a character's name or picture, usually held by a publisher or studio for toys, games, software and entertainment (Frederick Warne, now Penguin, holds UK marks in Peter Rabbit's name and Beatrix Potter's drawings).
3. **Special statutory rights.** Only one matters here: Great Ormond Street Hospital's royalty on *Peter Pan* in the UK (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, section 301 and Schedule 6).

**Recommendations:**

- **Include:** the character is free in the UK; design it from the public-domain sources listed.
- **Include with care:** free, but a famous later version (usually Disney's) must be avoided, or there is a cultural question; the "Design from / avoid" column says how.
- **Leave out:** a right survives or the position is doubtful. Following the workplan, doubtful means out.

### Rules for every commissioned design

These go into the design brief (WP3.6) and the contract (17.9, the artist's warranty):

- Work only from the public-domain sources named in the table: the original text, and illustrations whose illustrator died before 1956. Keep a list of the references used, with dates; it is part of the delivery.
- Never look at, trace or "refer to" a film, TV, game or modern book version while designing. If a design ends up close to a famous version anyway (a blue Alice dress with a white pinafore and black hairband, as in Disney's 1951 film), change it.
- Never use a character's name or picture in the app-store listing, the website's headlines or marketing as if StoryStage were a licensed product. Inside the app, the name is just the character's name.
- A design of a real culture's figure (Anansi, the Monkey King) is reviewed by someone from that tradition before it is accepted.

## 3. What the owner does

1. **Choose the shortlist** (12 to 20) with the curriculum mapping (WP16a), from the Include and Include with care rows.
2. **Run the register checks** for the shortlisted names in classes 9, 28 and 41 (the UK IPO's [search for a trade mark](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark), or the attorney from 17.6 in the same session). A live mark held by an active rights holder for software, games or entertainment moves the row to Leave out, or to legal advice.
3. **Take legal advice** on any row you want to keep that says "subject to the register check" and finds a live mark, and on Peter Pan if you want to ask GOSH.
4. **Fill in the Decision column** (include, leave out, or ask), and date it. Session D then turns the decisions into the design brief's character list for WP3.6.
