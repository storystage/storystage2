# Wales: Curriculum for Wales (WP16.3)

- **Workplan:** 16.3, "Curriculum for Wales areas of learning and experience (science and technology; languages, literacy and communication; expressive arts) and the cross-curricular digital competence framework".
- **Checked:** 25 October 2026, against the Curriculum for Wales guidance on Hwb (below). Method, fit levels and ids: [README.md](README.md). Features, activities and arc templates: [features.md](features.md).
- **In force:** Curriculum for Wales, from 2022 and in every year group by 2026; no replacement planned.
- **How Wales is written:** each area of learning and experience has a few **statements of what matters**, which are mandatory, and **descriptions of learning** at five progression steps, which are guidance written as "I can …" statements. The rows below cite the statement of what matters and the progression step, and paraphrase the description. Schools design their own curriculum from these, so a mapping like this one helps teachers plan rather than ticking off fixed content.

## Progression steps and ages

Progression steps are reference points, not year groups: roughly ages 5 (step 1), 8 (step 2), 11 (step 3), 14 (step 4) and 16 (step 5). A learner aged 6 to 13 is working from step 1 towards step 4, so the rows below cover steps 1 to 4.

**Film is a discipline here.** Wales's Expressive Arts area spans five disciplines: art, dance, drama, **film and digital media**, and music. No other nation names film-making as part of its curriculum at these ages, so this is StoryStage's most direct curriculum home.

**Welsh.** Welsh is compulsory in the Curriculum for Wales, and many learners are taught through Welsh. The languages descriptions are written for Welsh and English together. A StoryStage interface in English only would limit its use in Welsh-medium schools (see `mapping.md`, the questions for the owner).

## No touch point

Science and technology's sensors and actuators, networks, data storage and data-set descriptions (computation, steps 3–4); the living things and matter statements; the DCF's data and information literacy element and identity, image and reputation element; the languages descriptions on handwriting and spelling; dance and music in expressive arts.

## What Wales shows

- **Wales is StoryStage's most natural home.** Film and digital media is a discipline of Expressive Arts; the languages descriptions name animation from step 1; and the Digital Competence Framework asks for animation, key frames and transitions (creating), planning against success criteria (planning), and credit and copyright (digital rights, licensing and ownership).
- **StoryStage's design choices are curriculum requirements here.** Storyboard first (decision 0012) is the DCF's "plan before starting"; the storyboard check is "evaluate against success criteria"; licence capture and film credits (WP3.12, 11.4) are "digital rights, licensing and ownership".
- **Computation fits strongly from step 2 to step 4** (algorithms, prediction, conditions, loops, debugging, test plans).
- **The Welsh-language question matters more than anywhere else** (see `mapping.md`).
- **For the age decision (0030):** the descriptions fit from step 1 (about age 5) to step 4 (about 14), so 6–13 holds.
