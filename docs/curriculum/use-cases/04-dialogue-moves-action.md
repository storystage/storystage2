# 4. Dialogue that moves the action on

Learners write an original five-part story whose dialogue reveals character and drives the plot, direct how each line is performed, and improve the film from peer feedback.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ages** | 9–11 (England Years 5–6, Scotland P6–P7, Wales step 3, Northern Ireland P6–P7) |
| **Template** | 5-part (set-up, conflict, challenge, climax, resolution) |
| **Time** | Four lessons of 50–60 minutes |
| **Groups** | Pairs |
| **Prompter** | Explain only. The story coach asks "What does she want? What stops her?"; it never writes lines |
| **Curriculum** | `EN-EN-UKS2-1`, `EN-EN-UKS2-2`, `EN-EN-UKS2-3`, `EN-EN-SL-2`; `SC-ENG-x-31`, `SC-ENG-x-03`, `SC-EXA-x-14`, `SC-EXA-x-15`, `SC-LIT-x-23`; `WA-LLC-EX-3`, `WA-EA-RE-2`, `WA-EA-CR-3`; `NI-LL-KS2-W1`, `NI-AR-KS2-DR`, `NI-LL-KS2-TL3` |

## Learners will

- write dialogue that shows what a character is like without saying it;
- use dialogue to move the action from one part of the story to the next;
- direct a performance: the voice, the timing, the pauses and the gestures;
- give, receive and act on feedback.

## Lesson 1: two characters who want different things

1. Show a short scene where two characters argue. Ask: what does each want? How do we know?
2. Pairs write a logline and a synopsis for their own five-part story with two or three characters.
3. Add scene cards for the five parts, with headings and frames.

## Lesson 2: lines with a job

1. For each scene, write the lines. Rule: every line must either show character or move the story on; cut any that do neither.
2. Pairs read their scripts aloud in role and mark where a pause or a gesture would help.

## Lesson 3: direct the performance

1. Set the stage and code the scenes: each character **says** their line with a chosen voice; add **delay** blocks for pauses, and gestures (turn a joint: the head, an arm) at the marked moments.
2. Rehearse. Is the pause in the right place? Does the gesture land with the line?
3. Check each scene against the storyboard.

## Lesson 4: feedback and the final cut

1. Pairs swap films in the Screening room and leave two notes: one strength and one improvement, linked to a scene.
2. Act on at least one note, then explain to the class what changed and why.
3. Export with captions.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** use the 3-part template and two characters; give sentence starters for lines.
- **Stretch:** a scene where a character says one thing and their actions show another (subtext).

## What to look for

- Dialogue that reveals character and advances the action (England Years 5–6, writing composition).
- Deliberate choices of pace, pause and gesture (`ENG 2-03a`; Northern Ireland KS2 drama).
- Feedback that is specific, and changes made because of it (`EXA 2-15a`; Wales responding and reflecting).
