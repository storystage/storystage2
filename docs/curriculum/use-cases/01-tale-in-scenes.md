# 1. A traditional tale in scenes

Learners retell a traditional tale as a short film of five to seven scenes, then program the characters to move and speak.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ages** | 6–8 (England Years 1–3, Scotland P2–P4, Wales steps 1–2, Northern Ireland P3–P4) |
| **Template** | Traditional tale (`tale`): once upon a time, a problem, three tries, it works, happily ever after |
| **Time** | Four lessons of 45–60 minutes |
| **Groups** | Pairs or threes, one device each; the teacher leads the first lesson on the board |
| **Prompter** | Off works. Explain only if available: the story coach asks the questions in each part |
| **Curriculum** | `EN-EN-Y1-1`, `EN-EN-Y1-2`, `EN-EN-Y2-3`, `EN-CO-KS1-1`, `EN-CO-KS1-2`, `EN-CO-KS1-3`; `SC-ENG-x-31`, `SC-TCH-1-14`, `SC-TCH-1-15`; `WA-LLC-LI-1`, `WA-LLC-LI-2`, `WA-ST-CO-2`, `WA-DCF-PSM`; `NI-LL-KS1-TL1`, `NI-LL-KS1-R1`, `NI-ICT-EXPLORE`, `NI-MA-KS1` |

## Learners will

- retell a familiar tale in the right order, with a beginning, a problem and an ending;
- describe the setting and the main character of each scene;
- give a character a short sequence of instructions, predict what it will do, and fix it when it doesn't.

## Before you start

- Choose a tale the class knows well, with a clear "three tries" (The Three Little Pigs, Goldilocks, The Enormous Turnip, The Gingerbread Man).
- Check that Backstage has the characters, or decide on stand-ins (a labelled "wolf" works fine).
- Prepare a class production on the board to model with.

## Lesson 1: the story in parts (Storyboard)

1. Retell the tale together. Stop at each part of the template and ask its question ("Who is the story about? Where do they live?").
2. Model on the board: the title, then a one-sentence logline in the class's words ("A little pig wants a safe house, but a wolf keeps blowing them down"). Choose the **Traditional tale** template.
3. Pairs make their own production: title, logline, template.
4. Each pair adds a scene card for each part, with a heading (place and time: "The wood, morning").

## Lesson 2: frames and lines (Storyboard)

1. For each scene, drag sketches into a frame: who is there, and which way they face.
2. Write one or two lines of script for each scene: what the characters say. Younger learners can use voice input and edit what it types.
3. Pairs swap and check each other's storyboard against the tale: is anything missing, or out of order?

## Lesson 3: set the stage and code (Production stage)

1. Open the first scene. StoryStage offers to set the stage from the storyboard: accept it.
2. Model three blocks: the pig **moves forward**, **turns** to face the wolf, **says** its line.
3. Before pressing Rehearse, each pair predicts out loud what will happen. Then they rehearse and compare.
4. In "three tries", show how **count 3 times** can repeat the wolf's huffing and puffing (a first loop).
5. End with **Check against the storyboard**, and fix one thing it finds.

## Lesson 4: screen it (Screening room)

1. Finish and check the remaining scenes.
2. Watch the whole film in the Screening room, side by side with the storyboard.
3. Pairs watch another pair's film and give one "I liked" and one "Next time" note.
4. Export the film with a title card and credits (first names or initials).

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** the teacher sets up the scene cards in advance; learners add frames and lines only. Use three scenes (the 3-part template) instead of five.
- **Stretch:** add a sixth scene that changes the ending; use **do together** so two characters move at once.

## What to look for

- The scenes are in order, and each has a setting and a character (English, literacy).
- The learner predicts what a block will do before rehearsing, and says what went wrong when it doesn't match (computing).
- The check against the storyboard comes up clean, or the learner can explain the difference.
