# 8. Credit where it's due

A short sequence, run alongside any of the other seven, in which learners find out who made the models in their film, what each licence allows, and how a film's credits respect other people's work.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ages** | 8–13 (England KS2–KS3, Scotland P5–S2, Wales steps 2–4, Northern Ireland KS2–KS3) |
| **Template** | Any; use the production the class is already making |
| **Time** | Two lessons of 45–60 minutes |
| **Groups** | Pairs |
| **Prompter** | Off |
| **Curriculum** | `WA-DCF-RIGHTS`, `WA-EA-CR-4`; `SC-TCH-x-02`, `SC-LIT-x-25`; `EN-CO-KS2-7`, `EN-CO-KS3-9`; `NI-ICT-ESAFE` |

## Learners will

- know that every model, sound and picture belongs to someone, even when it's free to use;
- read a simple licence (CC0, CC BY, "not sure") and say what it allows;
- give credit properly, and explain why it matters.

## Lesson 1: who made this?

1. Open Backstage and pick three models from the class's film. Each shows who made it, where it came from and its licence.
2. Explain three licences in plain words:
   - **CC0:** anyone may use it, no credit needed (but giving credit is still kind);
   - **CC BY:** anyone may use it, and must say who made it;
   - **not sure:** we don't know, so we keep it in class and don't share the film outside school.
3. Pairs find one model of each kind they can, and write who made it.

## Lesson 2: the credits

1. Export a short test of the film and look at the credits StoryStage generated: the CC BY models are named automatically.
2. Add the learners' own credits: who wrote, directed, coded (first names or initials, as the school allows).
3. Discuss: what if someone used your film without saying you made it? Why does a credit matter to the people who made the models?
4. If imports are allowed in the class (`backstage.import`), show how the import asks for the licence, and why "not sure" stops a film being shared outside school.

## What to look for

- The learner can say who made a model and what its licence lets them do (Wales's digital rights, licensing and ownership element, steps 2–4).
- The film's credits name the makers of every CC BY model, and the learners (`LIT 2-25a`, acknowledging sources).
- The learner explains why crediting and respecting licences matters, for themselves as creators too.
