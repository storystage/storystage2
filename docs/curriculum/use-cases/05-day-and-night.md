# 5. Day and night (and the solar system)

Learners make a short narrated explanation film with an animated model of the Earth and the Sun, then test their explanation against what they know.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ages** | 9–11 for day and night; 11–13 for the extension on seasons (England Year 5 and KS3, Scotland P5–S2, Wales step 3, Northern Ireland P5–P7) |
| **Template** | Explain how it works (`explain`): the question, what we see, why it happens, what it means |
| **Time** | Three lessons of 50–60 minutes, after the science has been taught |
| **Groups** | Pairs |
| **Prompter** | Off or Explain only. Explain mode can explain a block; checking the science stays with the teacher |
| **Curriculum** | `EN-SC-Y5-1`, `EN-SC-UKS2-1`, `EN-SC-KS3-1`, `EN-SC-KS3-2`, `EN-CO-KS2-1`; `SC-SCN-1-06`, `SC-SCN-2-06`, `SC-LIT-x-28`, `SC-TCH-2-14`; `WA-ST-FE-3`, `WA-DCF-PSM`; `NI-WAU-KS2-1`, `NI-ICT-EXPRESS` |

## Learners will

- explain day and night as the Earth turning on its axis, not the Sun moving round the Earth;
- build and code a simple model that shows it;
- write a short, accurate narration, and judge whether the model and the words agree.

## Before you start

- Teach the science first. This sequence is for explaining and checking understanding, not introducing the idea.
- Backstage needs a sphere for the Earth and a light for the Sun (Backdrops has the lighting). A small person standing on the Earth helps show "where it's day".
- **StoryStage animates; it doesn't simulate.** A wrong model looks as smooth as a right one. Say so to the class: that is why we check.

## Lesson 1: the question and the plan

1. Ask the question: "Why do we have day and night?" Collect ideas, including the wrong ones.
2. Choose the **Explain how it works** template. Write the scene cards: the question, what we see (the Sun "rises" and "sets"), why it happens (the Earth turns), what it means.
3. Storyboard the frames: where the camera is, and where the Sun and Earth sit.

## Lesson 2: build the model

1. Set the stage: the Sun's light on one side, the Earth in the middle, a person on its surface.
2. Code the Earth to **turn** a full turn slowly, with **do together** so the narration plays while it turns.
3. Write the narration for each scene. A narrator character (or a caption-only title) speaks the lines with an on-device voice; captions are always on.
4. Predict: when the person faces away from the Sun, what does the narration say? Rehearse and check.

## Lesson 3: test the explanation

1. Pairs swap films and check the science against a short checklist: Does the Earth turn, not the Sun? Is one turn one day? Does the narration say the same as the model shows?
2. Fix anything the checklist finds, then export.

## Extension (ages 11–13): seasons

Tilt the Earth's axis and move it round the Sun over a "year", and show why the person's day is longer in summer. Use the same four parts.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** give pairs the stage already set; they code the turn and write the narration.
- **Stretch:** add the Moon and its phases (Scotland `SCN 1-06a` relates the sun and moon to a day, a month and a year).

## What to look for

- The model and the narration both show the Earth turning; the learner can say why the Sun appears to move (science).
- The narration explains, in order, with cause and effect (`LIT 2-28a`; England, working scientifically).
- The learner spots and corrects a mismatch between the model and the words.
