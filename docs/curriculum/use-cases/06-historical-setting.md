# 6. Step into the past

Learners recreate a historical setting and a day in someone's life there, using real museum objects, and compare it with their own lives.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ages** | 6–9 (England KS1–lower KS2, Scotland P2–P5, Wales steps 1–2, Northern Ireland KS1–P5) |
| **Template** | 3-part (beginning, middle, end): then, what happened, what was different. The history template (`recount`) is held for now |
| **Time** | Three lessons of 45–60 minutes, as part of a history topic |
| **Groups** | Pairs or threes |
| **Prompter** | Off. If used, Explain only, for questions about how to use StoryStage, not about history facts |
| **Curriculum** | `EN-HI-AIM`, `EN-HI-KS1-1`, `EN-HI-KS2-1`; `SC-SOC-1-03`, `SC-SOC-1-04`, `SC-LIT-x-28`; `WA-HU-EV-3`; `NI-WAU-KS2-2`, `NI-LL-KS1-TL2` |

## Learners will

- use evidence (objects, pictures, sources) to describe a place and time in the past;
- recreate a setting and a short sequence of events from someone's day;
- compare daily life then with their own, and say how they know.

## Before you start

- Choose a period the class is studying (a Roman fort, a Viking farm, a Victorian classroom, a medieval castle, or a local historical person or place).
- Look in Backstage's **Props** and **Set** for museum scans from the period (WP3.7). Where nothing fits, learners place a labelled stand-in ("butter churn") and note what the real object looked like.
- Have sources ready: pictures, a museum visit, an object handling box.

## Lesson 1: what do we know?

1. Look at the sources. What do they tell us about where people lived, what they wore, what they used?
2. Write the logline: "A Roman soldier's day at the fort." Make three scene cards: morning, midday, evening.
3. For each scene, list the objects the evidence shows, and find them in Backstage.

## Lesson 2: build the setting

1. Set each scene: the backdrop, the set and the props, placed where the evidence suggests.
2. Code a simple sequence: the person walks to a place, uses an object, speaks one line about what they're doing.
3. Check against the storyboard.

## Lesson 3: then and now

1. Screen the films. For each one, ask: what is the same as our day, and what is different?
2. Add a final title or line in each film that says how we know one thing (from a source).
3. Export, with credits that name the museums whose objects were used. StoryStage generates those credits from Backstage.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** one scene only, with the setting prepared by the teacher.
- **Stretch:** show the same place at two different times, and what changed.

## What to look for

- The setting draws on the evidence, and the learner can point to where each choice came from (`SOC 1-04a`; Wales's humanities descriptions).
- The comparison with today is specific.
- The learner understands that the film is a reconstruction: our best picture from the evidence, not a recording.
