# 3. Myths and legends

Learners retell, or invent, a myth or legend as a quest in four parts, with settings, characters and a hero who changes.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ages** | 8–11 (England Years 4–6, Scotland P5–P7, Wales step 3, Northern Ireland P5–P7) |
| **Template** | Myth, legend or quest (`myth`): the call, the journey, the trial, the return |
| **Time** | Five lessons of 50–60 minutes |
| **Groups** | Threes: a writer, a director and a programmer, swapping each lesson |
| **Prompter** | Explain only or Plan only. The story coach asks about the hero's goal and the trial, and never writes the story |
| **Curriculum** | `EN-EN-LKS2-2`, `EN-EN-LKS2-4`, `EN-EN-UKS2-1`, `EN-EN-UKS2-2`, `EN-EN-UKS2-4`, `EN-HI-KS2-1`, `EN-HI-KS2-2`; `SC-ENG-x-31`, `SC-ENG-x-19`, `SC-LIT-x-26`; `WA-LLC-LI-2`, `WA-LLC-LI-3`, `WA-EA-CR-3`; `NI-LL-KS2-TL1`, `NI-LL-KS2-R1`, `NI-LL-KS2-W1` |

## Learners will

- identify the parts of a quest in a myth they have read;
- plan a film that keeps that structure, with clear settings and characters;
- write and stage dialogue that shows what the hero wants;
- show how the hero has changed by the end.

## Choosing a myth

Pick one the class is studying, or let groups choose from a short list that reaches beyond Greece: Perseus and Medusa; Theseus and the Minotaur; Beowulf and Grendel; Fionn mac Cumhaill and the Giant's Causeway; the Children of Lir; Branwen (the Mabinogion); Anansi and the sky god's stories. Myths from living traditions are told with respect: use a version from that tradition, and talk about where the story comes from.

## Lesson 1: the shape of a quest

1. Read or tell the myth. Map it onto the four parts on the board: the call, the journey, the trial, the return.
2. Groups write a logline in one sentence: who wants what, and what stands in the way.
3. Choose the **Myth, legend or quest** template; add a scene card for each part, with a heading.

## Lesson 2: settings and characters

1. For each scene, choose the backdrop and the set (from Backstage), and describe the setting in the scene heading.
2. Build the frames from sketches: where the hero stands and what they face.
3. Write a sentence for each main character: what they want. Use these sentences when writing their lines.

## Lesson 3: the script

1. Write the dialogue for the call and the trial. Each line must show what a character wants or fears.
2. Read the script aloud in role around the group, then trim any line that doesn't move the story on.

## Lesson 4: stage and code

1. Set the stage from the storyboard, then code each scene: entrances, movement, the characters speaking their lines.
2. For the trial, use **do together** so the hero and the monster act at once.
3. Check each scene against the storyboard.

## Lesson 5: screen and reflect

1. Watch the films in the Screening room, compared with the storyboards.
2. Each group answers: how has the hero changed between the call and the return? Where does the film show it?
3. Export with title, captions and credits. The credits list each model's source automatically.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** give groups the four scene headings; they add frames and lines. Use the shortest myth.
- **Stretch:** retell the same myth from the monster's point of view, keeping the four parts.

## What to look for

- The film keeps the structure of the myth, with a clear trial and return.
- Settings are described and shown, and each character's lines reveal what they want (English, literacy).
- The learner explains how the hero changes (reading, `ENG 2-19a`, Wales's literature descriptions).
