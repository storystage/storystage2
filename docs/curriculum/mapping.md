**What follows:**

- **The curriculum value doesn't depend on the Prompter.** The Prompter's features appear in only 8 places in the mapping, and no statement needs them. The browser version and classes without a key keep the whole curriculum case, which matters because the Prompter needs an adult's key (17.5).
- **The storyboard check (`F-CHK`, WP11.6) earns its place.** It serves 20 statements in all four nations, every one a strong fit. Session D suggests the owner treats it as a feature the pilot can't do without.
- **The Screening room's notes and the export** carry the "respond, evaluate, showcase" statements in every nation.
- **Credits and licences** are a strength to tell Welsh schools about, and a teaching point elsewhere.
- **The text view is a bridge, not a destination** (16.8, WP20). KS3 computing in England and Third level in Scotland ask for more than StoryStage gives.

## 6. Use cases to write in WP16b

16.6 asks for 6 to 10 classroom use cases. These candidates come from the strongest themes and cover all four nations and the 6–13 age range:

1. **A traditional tale in three scenes** (ages 6–7; themes 3, 4): retell a tale with the storybook cast, code who moves and speaks, check it against the storyboard. England Year 1–2, Scotland First level, Wales step 1–2, Northern Ireland KS1.
2. **Make it match the storyboard** (ages 7–9; theme 4): a computing lesson where the storyboard is the specification and the check is the test.
3. **Myths and legends** (ages 8–11; themes 2, 3): a myth from Greece, Ireland, Wales or Scotland as a five-part film.
4. **Dialogue that moves the action on** (ages 9–11; themes 2, 8): script and voice a scene, then screen and improve it.
5. **Day and night, or the solar system** (ages 9–11; theme 12): England Year 5 and Scotland `SCN 2-06a`.
6. **Recreate a historical setting** (ages 6–9; theme 13): Scotland `SOC 1-04a`, with museum scans.
7. **The same scene, staged two ways** (ages 11–13; themes 7, 9): Shakespeare at KS3, and Northern Ireland's adapting texts to different media.
8. **Credits and copyright** (ages 8–13; theme 11): Wales's digital rights element, with imported models and their licences.

## 7. The age range (decision 0030)

Every nation has strong touch points from the start of formal schooling to the end of lower secondary:

- **The youngest learners:** England Year 1 (age 5–6), Scotland First level (from P2), Wales step 1 (about 5), Northern Ireland KS1 (P3, age 6–7).
- **The oldest:** England KS3, Scotland Third level, Wales step 4 and Northern Ireland KS3, all to age 14, though the KS3 computing fit is weaker.

**Recommendation:** confirm **6 to 13**. Learners aged 6–7 need teacher-led use and reading support (voice input and captions, `F-VOX`). The KS3 computing gap is met by the pathway, not by stretching the age range.

## 8. Questions for the owner

1. **Confirm the age range** (0030), on the evidence in section 7.
2. **Interface languages.** Welsh is compulsory in Wales, and many Welsh learners are taught through Welsh. Scotland has Gaelic-medium schools, and Northern Ireland has Irish-medium schools (with their own KS3 "Irish with Media Education"). An English-only interface rules StoryStage out of those classrooms. S5 already keeps wording in one place; the question is whether Welsh is in the first release, release 1.1, or later.
3. **A licence for this folder,** so teachers can reuse and adapt the mapping and the lesson outlines (decision 0025 left documentation and curriculum material to WP17). CC BY 4.0 is the usual choice for teaching material.
4. **Remapping.** England's draft programmes, Scotland's framework and Northern Ireland's Curriculum 2028 will all land between late 2026 and 2028 (README, "Reform watch"). Session D remaps each one when it is published.
