# Northern Ireland: the Northern Ireland Curriculum (WP16.4)

- **Workplan:** 16.4, "the Northern Ireland Curriculum, covering language and literacy, the arts, and the cross-curricular skill Using ICT".
- **Checked:** 25 October 2026, against the statutory minimum content on legislation.gov.uk and CCEA's guidance on Using ICT (below). Method, fit levels and ids: [README.md](README.md). Features, activities and arc templates: [features.md](features.md).
- **In force:** the Northern Ireland Curriculum, statutory since 2007. A draft Northern Ireland Curriculum Framework 2028 was consulted on until 30 September 2026 (README, "Reform watch").
- **How it is written:** the minimum content each key stage must teach is law: Schedule 2 to the Education (Curriculum Minimum Content) Order (Northern Ireland) 2007, set out as "pupils should be enabled to …" lists for each area of learning. Rows cite the key stage, the area and the strand, and paraphrase the item.

**Where coding lives.** Northern Ireland has no separate computing subject before Key Stage 4. Coding sits in the cross-curricular skill **Using ICT** (its "Explore" requirement covers computational thinking and coding), in **mathematics** (programmable devices at KS1, a first programming language at KS2), and in KS3 **technology and design** (computer-based control). CCEA lists the kinds of ICT children should experience as publishing, presenting, **coding**, managing data, art and design, music and sound, and **film and animation**, and publishes "Digital Storytelling: Film and Animation" desirable features and tasks.

**Media education is statutory at KS3.** KS3 English is "English with Media Education" (and Irish with Media Education in Irish-medium schools), with the moving image named throughout.

## No touch point

Music at every key stage; spelling, grammar and handwriting in language and literacy; the physical making in art and design (printmaking, textiles, ceramics); the manufacturing strand of KS3 technology and design.

## What Northern Ireland shows

- **Using ICT is the strongest single fit.** Its Explore and Express requirements, with "film and animation" and "coding" among CCEA's named kinds of ICT, describe the whole StoryStage journey, and Evaluate and Exhibit match the Screening room and export.
- **KS3 English with Media Education** fits strongly: moving image, adapting texts to different media, and presenting in different media are statutory.
- **Drama fits from KS2:** freeze frames and tableaux map onto storyboard frames, and KS3 names puppets and electronic media for characterisation.
- **Coding is spread across subjects** (Using ICT, maths, KS3 technology and design), so the teacher guide should show NI teachers where StoryStage's coding counts, rather than pointing to a computing subject.
- **Cú Chulainn:** CCEA already publishes KS2 units with animations on the life and times of Cú Chulainn, which supports including him in the storybook cast, with the care noted in `docs/legal/storybook-characters.md`.
- **For the age decision (0030):** Key Stages 1–3 (ages 6–14) fit, so 6–13 holds.
